# Final application feasibility decision

15 October 2026 — **NO-GO for advancing the current within-band SIMD direction as our acceptance-focused IAC 2027 paper. Do not initiate the physical hardware campaign.**

The bounded investigation found real spacecraft compression problems, but no documented application that establishes a distinctive, scientifically meaningful advantage for this execution method after the applicable alternatives. This is a project-selection decision, not a proof that the method can never win on any processor or future mission. The absence of physical measurements is not the reason for rejection.

This is an AI-assisted critical assessment, not independent external human peer review. No acceptance probability or guarantee is implied. The earlier REVISE record and physical pilot remain historical artifacts; this decision supersedes their continuation recommendation.

## Scope and decision standard

The [frozen protocol](protocol.md) limited this final audit to four application cases already relevant to this topic: HYPSO, EyeSat, the published LandIS study, and PROBA-V/Vegetation. They are application checks within the current topic, not proposed replacement topics. No further application search, codec implementation campaign, algorithm modification, new benchmark selection or performance simulation was undertaken. The new work consists of primary-source investigation, applicability analysis, arithmetic, and integrity checks of existing evidence.

A useful exact transformation need not be a revolutionary mathematical result. However, our proposed contribution requires an experimentally credible operating region where preserving the adaptive predictor through this execution method improves a relevant processing/resource trade-off over the best applicable conventional alternatives. Correctness, a new instruction set, and superiority over a sequential reference are insufficient by themselves.

The qualified prototype remains lossless unsigned 16-bit, zero damping and exponent offsets, Ω=19/R64/P3, full prediction, two supported local sums, sample-adaptive entropy coding, and within-band blocks capped at eight samples. These particular predictor settings and block cap are experimental choices, not established mission requirements. It is not a demonstrated near-lossless/hybrid-codec implementation or a general solution for every CCSDS profile.

## Application evidence

The following descriptions concern the cited publications' dates and configurations; they are not claims about every mission's current architecture in October 2026. Unreported source-to-output deadlines and compressor-specific memory allocations remain unknown.

**HYPSO: genuine flexible onboard processing, but substantial conventional parallelism.** The mission's [2023 processing paper](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/15/3756), §§3.2–3.4, documents a 12-bit camera, raw BIP arrival, unsigned-16-bit storage and online spectral sum-binning. Compression follows acquisition of a complete binned cube; typically 0.5 or 0.75 GB of DRAM is reserved for FPGA input/output. The CPU is dual Cortex-A9 with NEON, alongside an FPGA. Thus our binned DN experiments have a real processing context, but it is buffered and multi-band, not an imposed single-band streaming deadline. The [2024 IAC operations paper](https://rogerbi.folk.ntnu.no/publ/2024_Reconfig_IAC_paper.pdf), §3, reports in-orbit reconfiguration supporting the commonly used dimensions; fewer than 0.5% of captures in its stated post-update interval lacked hardware support. Inference: software fallback remains legitimate, but “the mission has no flexible hardware compressor” is an obsolete motivation. Conventional spectral execution and independent buffered contexts cannot be excluded. Our host spectral comparisons already favor the conventional implementation. No published requirement identified here establishes a need for the proposal's exact execution schedule.

**EyeSat: the strongest software-use case still does not justify our method.** The [2016 CNES/IRAP paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312197460_Lossless_Image_Compression_on_the_EYESAT_Nanosat), §§2–5, describes a 2048² Bayer sensor, monochromatic NIR/dark modes, 15-second exposures, mandatory lossless reconstruction, a target compression ratio of two, and a goal of sharing one active ARM core. Board memory is 1 GB DDR3 or 512 MB with EDAC; the compressor partition allocation and precise camera-to-memory schedule are unreported. The authors selected 64 independently decodable 256² segments and a custom nonadaptive predictor. They report roughly fivefold acceleration over their standard implementations, a 1–8% compression-efficiency penalty, and mission approval, despite falling slightly short of the ratio target. CCSDS 122 performed poorly in their tested Bayer comparison. Inference: this is a credible need for efficient lossless software, but neither exact adaptive weights nor one continuous compression context is mandatory. An approved simpler alternative and explicit segmentation already address the problem. No EyeSat flight-format input or target comparison establishes an additional useful point for our prototype. Exposure time is not a documented compressor scheduling slot; total board RAM is not its partition budget.

**LandIS: real per-band processing does not imply only one usable context.** The [NASA-hosted 2025 study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250008078/downloads/LandIS_compression_final.pdf), §§1,4,5, describes 26 channels, differing 10/20/60 m sampling, 12–15-bit depths, and a projected rate up to 1.8 Gbit/s. Its selected approach uses relative-error near-lossless CCSDS 123 with hybrid entropy coding, processing bands separately because their content is largely uncorrelated. It anticipates FPGA implementation. Inference: spectral prediction between co-registered bands may be inappropriate, but separate bands are themselves independent compression contexts; differing rates and dimensions require scheduling, not an automatic ban on batching. The paper does not establish a CPU-only, single-context memory/latency restriction or a bus-order requirement favorable to us. Its error-control mode is outside our qualified prototype. Extending to that mode is substantive work because reconstructed samples affect later predictions. A padded lossless replay cannot establish benefit for its actual requirement. This is the documented 2025 design study, not an assertion that all projected characteristics are final flight requirements.

**PROBA-V/Vegetation: few spectral bands, multiple detector streams, another operational codec.** The [VITO instrument/product manual](https://proba-v.vgt.vito.be/sites/probavvgt/files/Products_User_Manual.pdf), §1.2, documents three cameras, each with three 5200-pixel VNIR strips and three 1024-pixel SWIR strips. Its 12-second first-to-last-band observation offset concerns the same ground target's viewing geometry, not a documented 12-second bus-message delay. Its ground DN/HDF5 representation is not evidence of flight BIP/BIL wire ordering. The [mission image-quality paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261213565_PROBA-V_mission_for_global_vegetation_monitoring_standard_products_and_image_quality), §3.2, documents a data-rate budget requiring average compression around 3.5 and permitted compression noise. [ESA-hosted implementation material](https://indico.esa.int/event/130/contributions/712/attachments/773/949/DSCAL_CCSDS_ImageCompress_FPGA_SEFUW2016.pdf), slide10, identifies a CCSDS 122 RTAX FPGA implementation for PROBA-V. [ESA's platform description](https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Engineering_Technology/Proba_Missions/Proba-V_Boxing_clever) lists 16 Gbit flash mass storage; this does not specify compressor working RAM. Inference: preserving our lossless adaptive profile is not the stated operational objective. Multiple strips are potential contexts, but eighteen geometric strips are not proof of eighteen simultaneously usable SIMD lanes. Exact buffering and readout arbitration were not established. The historical four-band Vegetation compression corpus is a separate workload; it must not be relabeled as PROBA-V flight input or assigned PROBA-V requirements.

No audited case supplies the needed combination of profile fit, demonstrated value of adaptation, and an operational constraint that leaves the within-band execution method a distinctive opportunity after conventional execution choices. This does not prove that all alternatives have identical performance or zero integration cost.

## What the existing comparisons actually establish

| Applicable alternative | Evidence and fair interpretation |
|---|---|
| Conventional spectral SIMD | In the seven-band resident pilot, the proposal won 0/8 main configurations. Conventional adaptive methods used 8.99–17.22% less host time at the same compressed size for each profile. The earlier 120-band HYPSO campaign also favored native BIP spectral execution in all eight main configurations, by 1.172–1.358x. These are exploratory host results, not flight or RVV performance. |
| Component SIMD and strong scalar | The proposal retains a real measured advantage: 1.230–1.366x over the best tested sequential/single-band path on the seven-band pilot; 1.196–1.351x in the earlier HYPSO comparison. These implementations retain adaptation and do not require multiple images or bands. They are not proven optimal on every architecture. Winning here does not establish a spacecraft advantage when other applicable methods are faster. |
| Independent compression contexts | Existing stripe encodings include separate CCSDS headers. Across the four seven-band acquisitions, eight stripes with wide-neighbor prediction changed size by −0.682% to +0.220%. The wide-neighbor profile was also smaller than narrow-column for every selected full image. Parallel context SIMD was not implemented/timed; these figures prove neither a speedup nor negligible reset cost for another mission. Nevertheless, it is untenable to exclude independent contexts on the assumption that resets are always prohibitively expensive. |
| Calibrated fixed weights | Earlier-acquisition tables at Ω=12 produced outputs 1.126–9.328% larger than adaptive Ω=19 on held-out main seven-band cases. One calibration policy and a precision change do not establish that all fixed predictors fail. More fundamentally, spectral SIMD retains the same adaptive compression without this byte penalty. The observed value of adaptation is not evidence for our execution method. |
| Other operational codecs | Consider actual loss/error limits, decoder compatibility and integration costs. EyeSat's approved custom predictor is applicable there; lossy coding is not. PROBA-V's permitted lossy CCSDS 122 is applicable there. LandIS explicitly selects near-lossless error control; our lossless scope is inadequate for that use. Generic claims that CCSDS 122, JPEG-LS or any fixed predictor always wins would be unsupported. |

The exact numerical audit is in [application-arithmetic.json](evidence/application-arithmetic.json), derived without new performance trials from the retained [revision evidence](../ccsds123-revision/evidence/revision-analysis.json) and [earlier comparison evidence](../ccsds123-independent-reassessment/evidence/analysis.json). Eight configurations are four acquisitions with two local-sum settings, not eight independent missions or scenes. The Landsat files are calibrated Level1 products, transposed outside timing; they do not model focal-plane arrival. Frequencies were not locked. These limitations weaken generalization, but do not justify discarding consistently unfavorable comparisons or declaring an advantage by narrowing the comparator set.

## Arrival, memory, latency and resource checks

Independent contexts can occupy vector lanes on a single CPU core. They do not inherently require a second core. Adjacent rows within one adaptive context are not automatically independent: segmentation/reset or genuinely separate band/frame contexts must be explicit. Reset effects, headers, state arrays, address gathering, uneven stream lengths and entropy packing all carry costs. None is assumed free. Replacing an ARM mission processor with an RVV platform would be an experimental assumption, not a documented mission requirement; it also leaves conventional RVV implementations applicable.

Conversely, a prohibition on holding an entire cube would not automatically prohibit multiple contexts. As arithmetic only, eight 256² input tiles at two bytes/sample occupy 1 MiB; their two-row sample arrays occupy 8 KiB in total, before weights, entropy state, outputs, DMA and other allocations. These are not measured flight RAM or proof that eight tiles fit EyeSat's compressor partition. They show why “low memory” alone cannot establish a one-context requirement. The existing nominal HYPSO test cube is about 149.67 MiB in its actual unpacked u16 representation and was already buffered. No fabricated tiny memory allocation is introduced to exclude alternatives.

There is no demonstrated exclusive output-latency advantage either. In the retained Finnmark source-access trace, the proposal's implemented whole-row BIP staging required a source prefix of 81,961 samples before the first 1024 body bytes, whereas direct native-BIP spectral execution required 464. These are inferred causal source-prefix counts from resident execution, not measured DMA latency or intrinsic algorithmic bounds. A one-band-row output boundary is also available to sequential/component implementations. The within-band block itself needs its candidate samples before verification. No mission source/sink schedule found in this audit reverses these considerations.

Complete latency includes acquisition, unpacking/reordering, compression, transfers, packetization and output draining, with overlap only where the real implementation supports it. Equal adaptive output sizes imply equal byte volume, not equal packing or transfer time. Reset/fixed/other-codec size changes must include their stream envelopes and decoder requirements. Energy requires the measured platform power over that full operation; CPU ns/sample alone cannot establish image-level energy savings. Our current evidence contains no physical time, energy, cache/bus contention or mission-level latency result. It also provides no WCET guarantee: verification and replay are data-dependent.

## Scientific contribution and skeptical assessment

The candidate-specific execution idea remains technically credible. Its multistep sign speculation, candidate-weight construction, exact integer checks and scalar replay differ from simply changing the hardware. But its building blocks are established: exact block adaptive filtering, prefix computations and guarded speculation are prior techniques. [Sánchez et al.,2022](https://deic.uab.cat/~jbartrina/docs/articles/2022_10-IEEE_GRSL_IUMA_UAB.pdf) already analyze the CCSDS feedback dependencies and speculative weight-update execution. [Barrios et al.,2024](https://deic.uab.cat/~jbartrina/docs/articles/2024_01-IEEE_GRSL.pdf) already study parameter-controlled update bypass and weight selection. Their stored primary texts remain in the project; this audit does not assert they contain the identical block implementation or that an identical publication cannot exist elsewhere.

Therefore the defensible possible novelty is a specific engineering transformation with a demonstrated useful operating region, not a new general mathematical principle. The correctness argument is conditional verification followed by induction; Renode consistency supports implementation validity within scope. The profile-specific Ω=12 bound is a stronger comparator check, not a novel research theorem. None of these facts alone makes an original, practically consequential spacecraft paper.

The decisive skeptical objection is: **what relevant design decision changes because of this method, after allowing conventional adaptive parallelism and mission-approved compression choices?** This audit did not find a source-supported answer. Calling limited band count a requirement, imposing one continuous stream or exact byte interleaving, or transferring a different mission's memory/deadline into our benchmark would manufacture that answer. Further tuning might improve a kernel result but would not establish the missing application premise.

This is not a declaration that independent contexts or fixed weights dominate all future workloads. Their target costs and calibration quality remain partly unmeasured. Nor does the lack of measurements by itself invalidate an algorithm-engineering direction. Here existing operational or mission-approved choices address the historical HYPSO, EyeSat and PROBA-V problems; LandIS presents a different profile and a preliminary design, not a demonstrated completed flight compressor. Conventional adaptive methods win in the tested compatible buffered workloads, and the remaining proposed advantage depends on constraints we could not substantiate. That combination fails the project's scientific selection standard.

## Physical experiment and stopping decision

**No decisive physical experiment is justified by the present application evidence.** A board comparison could answer whether this kernel is faster on that board. It cannot establish that a mission requires its particular trade-off, exclude independent contexts without a real arrival/RAM contract, or turn established techniques into high mathematical originality. Do not port near-lossless mode, add new sensors/codecs, or optimize every comparator simply to continue this direction.

Physical timing, energy and full-system integration remain uncertainties, not invented failures. A justified reopening would require new external evidence: an actual payload data/profile and source/sink/memory budget, a concrete reason the ordinary alternatives do not already cover its useful trade-off, and an application benefit that the existing exact method could falsifiably deliver. Those are missing premises, not another authorized search or implementation backlog. There is no basis to say that all prerequisites are complete and the physical tests can now proceed.

The final recommendation is **NO-GO for this IAC research direction; stop further optimization and physical-test preparation under its current claim.** Keep the working prototype and all positive and negative evidence. The topic fits onboard-software subject matter, but technical fit and correct code are insufficient grounds to pursue it as the strongest acceptance-focused paper. Separate IAF eligibility issues recorded earlier do not determine this scientific verdict.
